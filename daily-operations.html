The Daily Operations of Farmers

A farm does not run itself. Every meal begins with long hours of planning, physical work, and daily decisions. This page explains the main stages of farm work, using rice and vegetable farming as common examples in the Philippines.

The Farming Cycle in Order

  1. Planning: choosing what to plant, when to plant, and how much money is needed for seeds, fertilizer, and labor.
  2. Land preparation: clearing weeds, plowing, and leveling the soil. Rice fields also need water channels and dikes (small walls that hold water).
  3. Planting: sowing seeds or transplanting seedlings by hand or with simple machines.
  4. Crop maintenance: watering, weeding, applying fertilizer, and checking plants for pests and disease.
  5. Harvesting: cutting or picking the crop at the right time so it does not spoil or lose quality.
  6. After harvest: drying, sorting, storing, and bringing the produce to buyers.

Physical Demands and Time Commitment

  • Work begins early in the morning to avoid the hottest part of the day.
  • Farmers spend many hours bending, lifting, walking, and working under the sun or rain.
  • Crops need attention nearly every day, including weekends and holidays.
  • Busy periods, such as planting and harvest, require extra hands, so families and neighbors often help each other.

Equipment and Resources

Common tools and resources on a farm
Item What it is used for
Hand tools (bolo, hoe, sickle) Clearing, digging, and cutting crops.
Plow or hand tractor Turning and loosening the soil before planting.
Seeds or seedlings The starting material for every crop.
Fertilizer Adding nutrients so plants grow well.
Water source or irrigation Keeping plants supplied with enough water.
Sacks, baskets, and drying space Storing and handling the harvest.
