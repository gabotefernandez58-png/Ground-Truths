Uncontrollable Variables and Agricultural Challenges

Even when a farmer does everything right, many things can still go wrong. This page explains the problems that are outside a farmer's control.

Weather: Typhoons, Floods, and Droughts

The Philippines is hit by many typhoons each year. Strong winds can flatten crops, floods can drown fields, and long dry spells (droughts) can leave plants without water. A whole season of work can be lost in a few days.

Changing Market Prices and Production Expenses

Farmers pay for seeds, fertilizer, fuel, and labor before they know what price they will get at harvest. When many farmers harvest at the same time, supply is high and prices can fall. When expenses rise but selling prices do not, income shrinks or disappears.

Pests and Crop Diseases

Insects, rats, snails, fungi, and plant diseases can damage crops at any stage. Farmers must watch their plants closely and act quickly, which often means spending more on treatment.

Transportation and Supply Chain Difficulties

After harvest, produce must travel from the farm to markets. Bad roads, high fuel costs, and long distances can delay delivery. Fresh vegetables and fruits spoil quickly, so delays mean losses. Middlemen between the farm and the consumer also affect how much the farmer finally earns.
